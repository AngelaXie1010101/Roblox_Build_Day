For schools and learning communities

Bring game creation into the school day.

Roblox Build Day is a structured, beginner-friendly hackathon program. The public challenge runs Monday through Friday, and schools can adapt the same learning experience into a school day, weekend sprint, or after-school format.

Program snapshot

Flexible, supervised, and designed for first-time creators.

Build Day can support STEM enrichment, advisory programming, after-school clubs, or a schoolwide creativity challenge.

Beginner-ready No coding or Roblox Studio experience required.
Project-based Students create, test, revise, and present a playable experience.
Flexible format Run as a weekend sprint, one day, or after-school sessions.
Clear outcomes Finished project, reflection, peer feedback, and showcase.
STEM

Connected to learning goals

Students practice computational thinking, iteration, testing, problem-solving, communication, and creative design.

Fit

Easy to schedule

The program can fit into a regular school day, a digital media block, a weekend intensive, or an after-school club.

Make

Turns interest into creation

Students use a platform many already recognize as a bridge into building, testing, and explaining their own ideas.

Flexible implementation models

Sample schedules for every school format.

Schools can use the full Build Day learning sequence in the format that best fits their calendar: a single school day, a weekend sprint, or shorter after-school sessions.

One school day

Structured enrichment day

Best for STEM days, advisory programming, digital media classes, or a schoolwide creativity block.

8:30-9:00

Kickoff and Theme Reveal

Introduce the challenge, examples, expectations, and learning goals.

9:00-10:15

Roblox Studio Mini Lesson

Learn workspace basics, parts, templates, testing, and saving.

10:30-12:00

Guided Build Sprint

Create the core of a simple game individually or with a team.

1:00-2:00

Test and Improve

Peer test, fix confusing moments, and add small improvements.

2:00-2:45

Showcase and Reflection

Demo projects and explain what students built and changed.

Shorter sessions

After-school sequence

Best for clubs, library programs, advisory groups, or classes that meet in shorter blocks.

Session 1

Theme and Studio Setup

Introduce the challenge and help students open or install Studio.

Session 2

Beginner Build Activity

Students learn parts, movement, templates, and playtesting.

Session 3

Core Game Build

Students create the goal, obstacle, scene, or activity.

Session 4

Improve and Test

Students gather peer feedback and refine the playable experience.

Session 5

Share and Reflect

Students present their project and explain what they learned.

Sample lesson plan

Build a mini challenge game.

Learning Goals

Students understand game objectives, user feedback, testing, iteration, and basic computational logic.

Project Task

Create a simple obstacle or adventure course with a clear start, goal, and win condition.

Facilitator Role

Support checkpoints, ask design questions, help students test, and encourage thoughtful revisions.

Assessment

Use a simple rubric for completion, creativity, usability, reflection, teamwork, and iteration.

Evidence of learning

Students finish with visible work.

  • A playable Roblox Studio project
  • A short design reflection
  • Peer feedback notes or testing observations
  • A final demo or brief presentation

School readiness

Simple setup requirements.

  • Computers that can run Roblox Studio
  • Internet access and supervised work time
  • A teacher, club sponsor, or facilitator
  • Optional family showcase invitation
